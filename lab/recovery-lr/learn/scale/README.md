# Gradient training with more passes and more train samples

The frozen recipe of `../recipe.ts` trains for 3 passes over 40,000 train samples, with settings that were tuned for JSON. Its row 9, the training and the count, is about 5 points of all three below the search and below the table filled by hand-written rules (see `../runs/README.md`). Two signs there suggested that the training is cut short: the largest share of a cell is only 0.78 to 0.79 after the three passes, and the trained tables run into the limit of 16 steps about 4,000 times on the validation split. This folder measures what the same gradient training reaches on the validation split when it gets more passes and more train samples, and with two other settings: a lower rate and another share at the start.

**What these numbers are, and are not.** This study changes settings on purpose, and the settings were chosen with the scores of the validation split in view. So its numbers are not rows of the frozen recipe and not results on unseen data. Only the train and the validation split were read. No held-back set was read and no test split was scored.

## Commands

Run from the repo root. `RECOVERY_LR_SCRATCH` is a folder outside the repo. `<scratch>` stands for the experiment's scratch folder, which is outside the repo and which other pages name `recovery-scratch`.

```sh
export RECOVERY_LR_SCRATCH=<scratch>/transfer/lr/build/runs
F=<scratch>/transfer/lr/runs/scale
bun lab/recovery-lr/learn/scale/run.ts --seed 1 --train 40000 --passes 30 --at 1,2,3,5,10,20,30 --search --folder $F          # seeds 1, 2, 3
bun lab/recovery-lr/learn/scale/run.ts --seed 1 --train 400000 --passes 3 --at 1,2,3 --search --folder $F                     # seeds 1, 2, 3
bun lab/recovery-lr/learn/scale/run.ts --seed 1 --train 160000 --passes 3 --at 1,2,3 --folder $F                              # seeds 1, 2, 3
bun lab/recovery-lr/learn/scale/run.ts --seed 1 --train 40000 --passes 10 --at 1,2,3,5,10 --learning-rate 0.01 --folder $F    # seeds 1, 2, 3
bun lab/recovery-lr/learn/scale/run.ts --seed 1 --train 40000 --passes 30 --at 15,20,25,30 --learning-rate 0.01 --folder $F   # seed 1 only
bun lab/recovery-lr/learn/scale/run.ts --seed 1 --train 40000 --passes 5 --at 1,2,3,5 --start-share 0.5 --folder $F              # seeds 1, 2, 3
bun lab/recovery-lr/learn/scale/run.ts --seed 1 --train 400000 --passes 0 --search --folder $F                                # seeds 1, 2, 3: the search without training
bun lab/recovery-lr/learn/scale/run.ts --seed 1 --train 40000 --passes 100 --at 40,60,80,100 --search --folder $F             # seed 1 only, stopped after pass 40
bun lab/recovery-lr/learn/scale/table.ts --folder $F                                                                          # the table below
```

`run.ts` is one training for one seed, with the recipe's trainer, twin, batch of 32, rate of 0.05 and share of 0.9 on the base's choice at the start unless an option says otherwise. It saves the snapped tables after each pass that `--at` names. The trainer has no schedule and draws the order of a pass from the seed and the pass, so the tables after pass 3 of a training of 30 passes are the tables of a training of 3 passes: the saved points after pass 3 give 84.25, 81.69 and 83.29 of all three for the seeds 1, 2 and 3, which are the numbers of row 9 for these seeds in `../runs/README.md`. Every saved point gets the recipe's count over the train samples of the run, and a record with the scores on the validation split and on the run's own train samples. With `--search`, the tables after the last pass also go through the recipe's search (4 passes) and count, over the train samples of the run.

The added terminals (`|` and `&`) are always picked from the first 40,000 train samples, so every run trains the same 5,102 cells whatever its count of train samples is.

## Can the dataset give more train samples soundly

Yes, for the validation split. `../../dataset.ts` makes sample `index` from program `index` and stores nothing, and a program is a validation program when a hash of its list of tokens is 0 modulo 9, whatever its index is. The first 400,000 train samples are the train programs among about the first 450,000 indexes. So a train program stays a train program, no validation program enters the train samples, and no program with the tokens of a validation program does either. The first 40,000 of the larger sets are the 40,000 of the recipe's runs. The held-back sets were made by other agents and were not read, so nothing was checked against them.

## What the numbers say

**In short: no.** Gradient training alone does not close the gap to the search or to the hand-written rules: not with 10 times the passes, not with 10 times the train samples, not with a lower rate and not with another share at the start. Every setting with the recipe's rate ends at 83 to 84 of all three and the lower rate ends below that, where the search without training has 87.40 over the same 40,000 samples (87.87 over 400,000) and the table filled by hand-written rules 88.03. The best whole samples of gradient training alone are 38.31, with 400,000 train samples, against 44.51 and 48.66. That best row costs 10 times the training time of the recipe (about 200 s alone on the machine in place of 20 s, by the 6.5 s that a pass over 40,000 samples takes) and a few minutes more to make the samples. The search that it does not reach takes 9 s.

- **More passes over the same 40,000 samples do not move gradient training.** All three is 83.08 after 3 passes, 83.21 after 5, 82.01 after 10, 83.02 after 20 and 83.62 after 30, and whole samples go from 36.33 to 35.11. Every one of these is inside the spread of the seeds at 3 passes (81.69 to 84.25). After 30 passes gradient training alone is still 3.8 points of all three and 9.4 of whole samples below the search without training, and 4.4 and 13.6 below the table filled by hand-written rules. It stays level with yacc error productions (83.08 and 37.10).
- **More train samples do not move all three either, and add 2 points of whole samples.** Three passes over 160,000 samples give 83.56 and 36.09, and three passes over 400,000 samples give 83.47 and 38.31, against 83.08 and 36.33 over 40,000. The run over 400,000 samples takes the same 37,500 steps of Adam as 30 passes over 40,000 samples (83.62 and 35.11), so at equal steps ten times the samples give the same all three and 3.2 points more whole samples. With more samples the seeds come closer together (83.30 to 83.74 over 400,000 samples, 81.69 to 84.25 over 40,000) and the tokens skipped at the limit of 16 steps fall from 3,904 to 3,259. The whole samples of 38.31 are still 6.2 below the search without training and 10.3 below the hand-written rules.
- **The training is not short of samples: it does not fit the ones it has.** In every row the score on the run's own train samples is 0.6 to 1.3 above its validation score, e.g. 84.11 against 83.47 over 400,000 samples and 84.67 against 83.62 after 30 passes over 40,000. The search reaches 89.5 on the 40,000 train samples with the same cells. So the tables that the training ends at are 5 points below what the cells can hold on samples that the training has seen 30 times.
- **The two signs of a training that is cut short do not go away.** The largest share of a cell rises slowly, from 0.785 after 3 passes to 0.847 after 30 and 0.853 over 400,000 samples, and the tokens skipped at the limit of 16 steps stay at about 3,300 to 4,200, where the hand-written rules skip 61. The score of the snapped tables jumps from one pass to the next, e.g. seed 2 has 82.25, 77.52 and 82.66 after the passes 9, 10 and 11, while the trainer's loss per code point falls little: 0.90 to 1.05 after pass 3, and 0.81 to 0.89 after pass 30. So 3 passes do not cut off a training that is on its way up: the training moves around one level. A reading of these numbers, which no run here tested: what the loss rewards while the cells hold mixes is not what scores well once every cell is snapped to one choice.
- **Two other settings give the same level or less.** A rate of 0.01 in place of 0.05 was tried because the jumps from pass to pass look like steps of Adam that are too large. The jumps do get smaller, but 10 passes reach 81.12 and 32.25, below what 3 passes reach at 0.05, with 5,719 tokens skipped at the limit. One training of 30 passes at this rate, for seed 1, has 79.63 after 15 passes, 80.32 after 20, 81.79 after 25 and 81.91 after 30, with 31.36 whole samples, where the same seed has 84.25 and 37.78 after 3 passes at 0.05. A share of 0.5 on the base's choice at the start in place of 0.9 was tried because a largest share of 0.78 after three passes says that many cells have not moved far from their start. It reaches the level after one pass and goes no higher: 83.38 and 36.75 after 1 pass, 83.32 and 37.67 after 3, 83.56 and 37.55 after 5, with about 3,000 tokens skipped at the limit.
- **The search after a longer training gives what it gives after the recipe's training, and a little more over 400,000 samples.** After 30 passes over 40,000 samples the search and the count give 88.02 of all three and 48.07 whole samples, against 88.20 and 47.91 for the same three seeds of row 10. After the 3 passes over 400,000 samples, with the search and the count over the same 400,000 samples, they give 88.67 and 49.75. That is 0.5 and 1.8 above the same three seeds of row 10, and 0.6 and 1.1 above the hand-written rules, with the lowest seed (88.04) level with the rules. But the gain is the search's and not the training's: the search and the count without training over the same 400,000 samples give 87.87 and 45.92, which is 0.5 and 1.4 above row 11 over 40,000 samples (87.40 and 44.51, with other seeds for the order of the search). So ten times the samples give the search about half a point of all three and 1.4 of whole samples with or without a training before it, and the training adds to the search what it adds in the recipe: 0.8 of all three and 3.8 of whole samples over 400,000 samples, 0.7 and 3.9 over 40,000. The search took 161 to 208 s over 400,000 samples on the loaded machine.

## What ran and what did not

All runs started between 16:09 and 16:34 on October 16 and the last one ended at 16:40, with 7 to 10 processes at once until 16:32.

- Ran, seeds 1, 2 and 3: 30 passes over 40,000 train samples, with points saved after the passes 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20 and 30, and the search and the count after pass 30. A command took 542 to 579 s, of which 448 to 485 s were training and 31 to 34 s the search.
